# Architecture Handbook — Comprehensive Feedback

> **Review record — remediated on 2026-07-14.** This file is retained as review history, not as a normative architecture source. The resulting accepted decisions and proposed gates live in `docs/decisions.md`.

Review of the Vantheon product and frontend architecture handbook as of commit `daa6b19`.
Scope: `README.md`, `PLAN.md`, `GEMINI.md`, `REVIEW.md`, `docs/*` (10 documents), and `.github/workflows/gemini-review.yml`.

---

## Verdict

This is an unusually disciplined architecture handbook. The authority model, provenance chain, and failure semantics are more rigorous than most shipped financial products, and the refusal to invent performance budgets before measuring is exactly right. The document set is also internally consistent to a degree that is rare at this size.

The problems are not sloppiness. They are **load-bearing gaps at the exact points where the product makes its safety claims**, plus a set of open questions that have escaped into doc footers instead of living in the decision ledger where the README says precedence is decided.

Four things need answers before implementation starts. One thing (agent safety) is missing entirely and is arguably the largest risk in an AI-authored trading product.

---

## What is genuinely strong

Worth naming, because these should not be traded away when addressing the gaps below.

- **The authority model.** "Reads arrive as snapshots and ordered patches; writes leave as typed commands; only an authoritative server patch changes business truth" (`docs/frontend-architecture.md:18`) is stated once and then held consistently across all ten documents. The corollary — "no client cache, connection, or subscription proves current authorization" — is the single best line in the handbook.
- **Pending vs. confirmed as a first-class distinction.** `docs/accessibility-and-motion.md:160-172` treats "an acknowledgement is not an execution" as an accessibility requirement, not just an engineering one. Most products discover this after an incident.
- **D-024 (separate pause and order-cancellation).** Correct, and correct for the stated reason. Combining them is the kind of thing that seems user-friendly until someone loses money.
- **No-trade as evidence.** `docs/domain-model.md:353` — "No-trade decisions are first-class evidence rather than missing activity." This shapes the whole Paper surface and it is the right call.
- **D-018 (budgets from baselines, not intuition).** Rare discipline. Keep it.
- **IndexedDB is explicitly never canonical**, and consequential commands are never queued offline (`docs/frontend-architecture.md:210`). This closes a failure mode that most offline-capable apps ship with.

## P0 — Blocking. Resolve before the first vertical slice.

### 1. "Eligible for Live" is never defined

The Live promotion gate appears in at least six places — `docs/state-diagrams.md:130-131` (`Assess Paper evidence eligibility` → `Eligible?`), D-007, the trust ladder (`docs/product-foundations.md:68-69`), `docs/information-architecture.md:185`, and Journey 2 Stage 7 — and is defined in **zero** of them.

This is the most safety-critical decision in the product. Right now the handbook says Live requires Paper evidence, and then declines to say what evidence counts. In practice that means whoever implements the promotion screen invents the criteria, or there are no criteria and the button is always enabled.

The same gap exists one rung down: `docs/information-architecture.md:117` gives "Evidence sufficient for simulated operation" as the trigger for `Launch Draft Vn to Paper`, also undefined.

**Needed:** a decision entry defining minimum Paper evidence — some combination of observation window, run count, regime coverage, and whether a strategy that has taken zero trades can be promoted at all (today, nothing prevents it). Even "eligibility is advisory, the user may always override with an acknowledged warning" is a legitimate answer. Silence is not.

### 2. What is in `Draft V6` at checkpoint `V6.0`?

`docs/domain-model.md:85` says launching Paper "Begin[s] Draft V6 at checkpoint V6.0." Nothing in the repository says whether V6.0 is a **copy of the frozen V5.2 content** or an **empty draft**. I grepped for it; the concept is absent.

This is not a detail. It determines the entire iteration loop:

- If V6.0 clones V5.2, then "Draft V6" is a confusing name for "the same strategy you already had," and the major-version bump communicates deployment separation rather than change — which is defensible, but must be said and shown in the UI.
- If V6.0 is empty, then launching Paper silently discards the user's working strategy, which would be a catastrophic first-session experience.

Everyone reading this doc is currently assuming the first. Write it down.

Related: invariant 3 (`docs/domain-model.md:463`) says each accepted semantic change set creates one checkpoint. `V6.0` is created by a Paper launch, not by a change set — an unstated exception to a rule presented as non-negotiable.

### 3. Checkpoint restore has no lifecycle

`docs/domain-model.md:182` says checkpoints are restorable and that "restoring one still happens through a validated change against the current Draft." The IA exposes a History surface with "restorable checkpoints" (`docs/information-architecture.md:57`).

But the Draft checkpoint state machine (`docs/state-diagrams.md:64-95`) has **no restore transition**, and no document answers the obvious question: if I am at V8.4 and restore V8.2, do I get V8.5 (containing V8.2's content), or does the draft rewind to V8.2? Does restore produce a change set that goes through semantic review like every other mutation?

Given how much of the handbook rests on "one checkpoint per accepted change set" and "restoring does not erase intervening provenance," this needs one paragraph and one added transition to diagram 2.

### 4. Agentic safety is entirely absent

`GEMINI.md` describes this as "an agentic financial application." The handbook contains **zero** mentions of prompt injection, hallucination, guardrails, model evaluation, adversarial input, or untrusted data. I searched; there are none.

The architecture is meticulous about the client never becoming authoritative over the server. It never asks the harder question: **what makes the Champion's proposals trustworthy?**

Concretely:

- The Champion reads market data, news, run logs, and portfolio state — all of which are inputs it does not control — and emits typed commands that change a strategy which may later control real capital. That is a prompt-injection surface with a direct path to money.
- The only human gate on a Champion-authored change is the semantic change review (`docs/information-architecture.md:248`), whose *content is authored by the same model that proposed the change*. If the Champion misunderstands a rule, it will also misdescribe it in the review. The review surface inherits the model's errors rather than catching them.
- `docs/domain-model.md:194` mentions capturing "Champion runtime, toolset, model configuration" for reproducibility. That is the only acknowledgement that the model is a moving part. There is no document on Champion behavior, tool authorization, or how you detect that a model upgrade degraded strategy quality.
- D-020 says "AI review and automated testing are evidence, not authority" — for *code review*. The same principle is never applied to the Champion itself, which is the product.

**Needed:** a `docs/champion-runtime.md` (or equivalent) covering untrusted-input boundaries, which tools the Champion may invoke and under what authorization, server-side validation of proposed change sets independent of the model's own explanation, and how Champion behavior is evaluated and regression-tested across model versions. This is the largest single gap in the handbook.

### 5. The decision ledger does not contain the open decisions

The README (`README.md:75-80`) makes the ledger precedence root #1. But `docs/decisions.md` contains 24 entries: 23 Accepted, 1 Deferred, and **0 Proposed** — despite the ledger itself defining "Proposed" as "requires an explicit decision before dependent work begins."

Meanwhile there are **22 `unresolved` markers** scattered across doc footers: 8 in `frontend-architecture.md`, 8 in `performance-simulation.md`, 6 in `accessibility-and-motion.md`, plus the viewport list in `information-architecture.md:316-323`. None has an ID, an owner, a date, or a revisit trigger. At least two of them gate the first vertical slice (identity/session mechanism; supported viewport).

The ledger also carries **no dates and no owners anywhere**. Every entry has a "Revisit when" trigger and nobody watching for it.

**Needed:** promote every `unresolved` item to a `P-0xx` Proposed entry with an owner and a blocking/non-blocking flag, and add `Date` + `Owner` fields to the ledger template. The scattered footers become links to the ledger.

## P1 — Significant gaps

### 6. D-015 (stock shadcn) and D-016 (WCAG 2.2 AA) are in unverified conflict

`docs/ui-component-architecture.md:242` makes "recognizable stock shadcn styling with **no bespoke theme work**" an MVP acceptance criterion. `docs/accessibility-and-motion.md:5` makes WCAG 2.2 AA the accepted target for every supported viewport.

WCAG **2.2** (not 2.1) adds 2.5.8 Target Size (minimum 24×24 CSS px) and 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured. Stock shadcn's default `text-muted-foreground` contrast and the hit areas on badges, dense table controls, and small buttons are precisely where these fail. The handbook asserts both goals and verifies neither, and D-015's acceptance criterion forecloses the obvious fix (adjust the tokens).

**Needed:** audit the stock tokens and default component sizes against 2.2 AA. Then either (a) record the token deltas required and amend D-015 to permit them, or (b) downgrade the accessibility claim to "2.1 AA plus documented 2.2 exceptions." Do not ship both claims unverified.

### 7. Paper comparison has no methodology

The product's central epistemic claim is "is V8 better than V5?" — and the handbook's answer is a prohibition, not a method: "Avoid declaring a winner solely from short-term return" (`docs/priority-user-journeys.md:332`), "disclose unequal observation windows" (`docs/domain-model.md:293`).

Comparing a 90-day Paper V5 against a 3-day Paper V8 is statistically meaningless, and disclosure does not make it meaningful — it just makes the meaninglessness legible. Since the entire trust ladder rests on this comparison, the handbook needs to say what a *supported* comparison requires (common window, minimum runs, regime overlap) and what the UI does when those conditions are not met.

### 8. No cost or latency model for the Champion

The performance work is exhaustive about frontend event-to-paint latency, measured in milliseconds. It never mentions that "time to first coherent Draft" — a named measure in `docs/product-foundations.md:161` — is dominated by **model inference time**, likely tens of seconds, and that iteration-loop cost is dominated by **token spend**, not render cost.

`docs/performance-simulation.md` models "chat token rate" as a load parameter for the *frontend*, which is the right thing for the frontend and the wrong thing for the product. The 356-line simulator will tell you the UI paints in 12ms while the user waits 40 seconds for the Champion. Both journeys' stated goal is speed; the dominant term in that speed is unmodeled.

### 9. Paper portfolios are unbounded

D-005 permits multiple coexisting Paper portfolios, each of which is a *persistent, scheduled, running* thing. Nothing in the IA gives the user an action to stop or archive one — `Stopped` exists as an operational state (`docs/state-diagrams.md:307`) with no user-facing path to it. After twenty iterations a user has twenty portfolios executing runs forever, and a comparison surface with twenty columns.

### 10. React `Activity` is experimental and the warm-tier design leans on it

`docs/frontend-architecture.md:192` and `PLAN.md:107` build the warm-Champion cache tier partly on React `Activity`. That API is not stable. The handbook correctly notes the effect-cleanup hazard but treats availability as given. Make the fallback (explicit unmount plus rehydrate from cached queries) the baseline and `Activity` the optimization.

### 11. The simulator is a large parallel project competing with the product

`docs/performance-simulation.md` specifies a contract-level backend simulator with recorded replay, seeded generation, a twelve-profile fault matrix, correctness oracles, and five CI tiers — before a single vertical slice exists, and before the backend contracts it simulates have been designed (the doc concedes this: "Exact payloads remain a later contract decision").

D-017 is correct in principle: measure before optimizing. But as scoped, this is weeks of work simulating an interface that does not exist yet, and it will need to be rewritten when the real contracts land. Scope it down to the smallest deterministic harness that supports Slice 1 (normal profile, one disconnect/backfill scenario, event-to-paint marks) and stage the rest behind real contracts.

## P2 — Consistency defects

### 12. `REVIEW.md` is stale and actively contradicts the accepted handbook

This is the most immediately actionable item. `REVIEW.md` is untracked, reviews a **superseded revision of `PLAN.md`** (its line citations no longer resolve — e.g. it cites "PLAN.md line 73" for the Next.js choice, which is now line 90), and its recommendations directly violate accepted decisions:

| `REVIEW.md` recommends | Contradicts |
|---|---|
| Color-coded Draft/Paper/Live taxonomy as the primary distinguisher (§2.2) | `docs/ui-component-architecture.md:135` and D-016 — never color alone |
| "Branch from current view (Draft V5.2 — starts a new branch)" (§Proposal 1) | D-009 and invariant 1 — no MVP branching |
| Conflict resolution offering "Apply local changes (overwrite the server draft)" (§3.3) | `docs/frontend-architecture.md:210` — "Never resolve a conflict by uploading the final local JSON snapshot over the server Draft" |
| A single "Emergency Circuit Breaker" button (§4.2) | D-024 — pause and cancellation are deliberately separate operations |

A stale review sitting at repo root, contradicting the handbook, is a liability: humans and AI reviewers alike will read it as current. Delete it, or move it to an `archive/` directory with a header stating it is superseded and which of its recommendations were rejected and why. Several of its concerns (blank-canvas onboarding, chat-target confusion) were in fact absorbed into the handbook — that history is worth one paragraph, not 216 lines.

### 13. The one-owner rule is violated in its own table

`PLAN.md:228` requires "every state domain must have exactly one declared owner." `docs/frontend-architecture.md:90` assigns viewed context and mutation target to "**Zustand or route-derived state**." That is two owners. Deep-link restore (`docs/information-architecture.md:83`) implies the router should win. Pick one.

### 14. Two competing glossaries

`docs/product-foundations.md:78-93` presents a canonical terminology table. `docs/domain-model.md:44-58` presents a different one. The first omits Deployment, Evaluation, Intent, Command, Decision, Order, Trade, and Outcome; the second omits Change set, Evidence, Paper launch, and Live promotion. Both are labeled canonical.

Additionally, one concept family carries four names across the docs: `Experiments` (IA nav), `Evaluation` (domain model), `quick evaluation`, `dry run`, and `backtest`. `Experiment` appears in the navigation hierarchy but in neither glossary.

**Needed:** one glossary, in one file, referenced by the other.

### 15. The README's core diagram implies Draft → Live

`README.md:35-36` draws both `H["Explicit Live review"] --> LD` and `D["Immutable Strategy Vn"] --> LD`. The second edge is technically defensible (Live pins an immutable strategy), but read as a flowchart it shows a path from a strategy to Live deployment that does not pass through Paper — the exact thing D-007 forbids. Route Live deployment solely through the review node, or annotate the edge.

### 16. No CI validation of the constraints the docs demand

`PLAN.md:228`: "All Mermaid diagrams must render successfully, terminology must remain consistent across documents, and every state domain must have exactly one declared owner."

None of these is checked. There is no markdown lint, no mermaid render check, no link checker, and no terminology check. The repo asserts constraints it does not enforce — and item 13 above shows one of them is already violated.

## Process and tooling

**The only quality gate has never run.** `gemini-review.yml` triggers on `pull_request` only, and all three commits went directly to `main`. The workflow has executed zero times. If PR review is the intended gate, the branch protection to force it does not exist.

**`gemini-2.5-flash` is under-powered for this repo.** The review task is cross-document invariant checking across 4,200 lines with a 25-turn budget. Flash will produce plausible surface commentary and miss exactly the class of defect listed in P0 above — as evidenced by the fact that the P0 items survived the multi-agent review that produced `REVIEW.md`. Use `gemini-2.5-pro` here, or treat the workflow as a smoke check and not as the gate `GEMINI.md` describes.

**`.agents/` (80 files) is untracked.** Decide: `.gitignore` it, or commit it as process history. Leaving it as untracked working-tree noise means it will eventually be committed by accident.

**`PLAN.md` should be demoted or deleted.** `README.md:66` already says it is "not the canonical source." But it is 241 lines of normative content duplicated from `docs/`, and it has *already drifted* — `REVIEW.md`'s dead line references are the proof. Its unique value is the validation-scenario list (`PLAN.md:204-228`), which deserves to become a traceability matrix mapping each scenario to the document that specifies it and the test that will prove it. The rest is a maintenance liability.

## Suggested order of work

1. **Answer the four blocking questions** (Live eligibility, `V6.0` seed content, restore semantics, agent safety) — the first three are a paragraph each; the fourth is a new document.
2. **Delete or archive `REVIEW.md`.** It takes five minutes and removes an active contradiction from the repo root.
3. **Migrate the 22 `unresolved` markers into the ledger** as `Proposed` entries with owners. Add `Date`/`Owner` to the ledger template.
4. **Audit stock shadcn against WCAG 2.2 AA** and reconcile D-015 with D-016.
5. **Fix the consistency defects** (one glossary, one state owner, the README diagram edge).
6. **Add the CI checks the docs already require** (mermaid render, link check, markdown lint), enable branch protection, and upgrade the review model.
7. **Re-scope the simulator** to what Slice 1 actually needs.

Items 1–3 and 5 are roughly a day of writing. They convert a handbook that reads as complete into one that actually is.
